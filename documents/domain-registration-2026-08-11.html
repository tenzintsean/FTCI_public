Registering ftcihotels.org

A step-by-step guide. About 20 minutes, one card payment, no technical knowledge needed. Please complete it today — several other things are waiting on it.

For Tsetan

What this is. The new FTCI Hotels booking website needs a web address of its own. We are registering ftcihotels.org.

Why it can't wait. The website's confirmation emails — the ones that tell a guest we've received their booking — can only be set up once we own the address. Until then no email can be sent, and email is how the whole booking system talks to guests and to hotel staff. This one task is holding up several others.

Note: ftcihotels.com is already registered and in use for the existing site. That is a separate conversation and does not block this. Registering the .org now lets everything else move forward today.

Before you start

Have these three things to hand:

  • A payment card that works for international online payments (the registry charges in US dollars). Expect roughly US $10–15 per year. Use FTCI's card if one is available — see the note below.
  • A shared FTCI email address that more than one person can open — for example the office address already in use. Please don't use a personal address. This is the single most important choice on this page; the note below explains why.
  • FTCI's official details: registered organisation name, postal address and a phone number. These go on the ownership record.
Why the shared address matters more than it looks. Every year the registrar sends a verification email, plus renewal reminders. If nobody opens them, the domain is suspended or expires — and the website and every email address on it stop working that day. Domains are lost to an unread reminder far more often than to anything dramatic. A personal address also means password resets go to one person; if they're travelling, unwell, or have moved on, nobody else can get in.

Same reasoning for the card: a personal card that expires or gets replaced makes auto-renewal fail silently, and the first anyone knows is the site going dark.
Good news: there is nothing to install and nothing to configure. You are buying a name and then handing us the keys. We do the technical part.

The steps

  1. Create a Cloudflare account (or sign in)

    Go to:

    https://dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up

    Use the permanent email address from the list above. Confirm the verification email Cloudflare sends you, then sign in.

    Why Cloudflare? They sell domains at exactly what the registry charges — no markup and no renewal price jump, which most sellers add. They are also where the new website itself will run, so keeping the name in the same place means one fewer account to manage.

  2. Open the domain registration page

    Once signed in, go straight to:

    https://dash.cloudflare.com/?to=/:account/registrar/register

    Or from the menu: Domain Registration → Register Domains.

  3. Search for the name

    Type exactly this into the search box:

    ftcihotels.org

    It should come up as available. We checked it this morning against the official registry.

    ⚠️ Stop here if either of these happens

    (a) It says the name is taken, or (b) Cloudflare doesn't offer .org or won't accept your card.

    Don't improvise and don't buy a different name. Message Rajesh with a screenshot and we'll switch to the backup plan immediately. A wrong name registered in a hurry is much harder to undo than a ten-minute delay.

  4. Choose the longest term offered — at least 3 years

    Pick 5 or 10 years if the option is there; 3 years at minimum.

    This matters more than the money saved. If a domain is ever allowed to lapse, the website and every email address on it stop working the same day — and someone else can buy the name. A longer term removes that risk for years at a time. The difference in cost is a few dollars.

  5. Enter FTCI's details as the owner

    The form will ask for a first and last name, and it is required. That is normal — the rules that govern domain names insist on a real, reachable person as the contact, even when an organisation owns the name. Put your own name in; it does not make you the owner.

    The field that decides ownership is “Organisation”. Fill it in.

    FieldWhat to enter
    Organisation ← the important one FTCI's full registered name. This is the field that makes FTCI the owner.
    First / last name Your own real name. Required by the rules; this is the contact person, not the owner.
    EmailThe shared FTCI address from the checklist
    Address & phoneFTCI's official postal address and number

    ⚠️ Do not leave “Organisation” empty

    An empty Organisation field means the person named becomes the legal owner — the one outcome we are trying to avoid.

    If you cannot find an Organisation field anywhere, look for “Company” or an expandable “Optional details” section first. If there truly isn't one, stop before paying and message Rajesh.

    Use real details throughout — verification emails go to the contact named here, and deliberately false information can get a domain suspended.

    Your personal details are hidden from public view automatically — Cloudflare redacts them at no cost.

    Why this matters. Whoever the ownership record names is the owner, legally. If it ends up naming an individual and that person later moves on, the organisation has to ask them for its own web address back — and if the relationship has soured, the realistic choices are to pay them or lose the name. With FTCI in the Organisation field, the domain belongs to FTCI regardless of who is managing it at the time.

    You are registering it on FTCI's behalf. The account is yours for now so we can move today rather than wait on paperwork — but the ownership record should say FTCI from the very first day, because changing it later is far more work than getting it right now.

  6. Do not add anything else

    You only need the domain name. If you are offered any of the following, decline all of them:

    • Web hosting — the site runs somewhere else already
    • Email hosting or mailboxes — we set this up differently, at no cost
    • An SSL certificate — included free
    • WHOIS / privacy protection — already included free
    • Website builders, SEO packages, security add-ons

    Cloudflare generally doesn't push these. If you end up on a different site because of the backup plan, you will see all of them — say no to every one.

  7. Pay, then check that auto-renew is ON

    Complete the payment. You should get a confirmation email within a few minutes.

    Then open the domain's settings and confirm Auto-renew is switched on. It usually is by default — please check rather than assume. Together with the multi-year term from step 4, this is what stops the name quietly expiring years from now when everyone has forgotten it needs renewing.

    Take a screenshot of the confirmation screen showing the domain name and the number of years.

  8. Give us access — this is the part that unblocks the work

    Registering the name isn't enough on its own; we need permission to add the technical records that make email work.

    In Cloudflare, go to Manage Account → Members → Invite, and invite Rajesh's email address with Administrator access.

    You stay in control. The account and the domain remain yours. An invitation only lets us add the settings the website needs, and you can remove it at any time.

  9. Send back four things

    • The confirmation screenshot
    • Which email address you used for the account
    • Confirmation that the invitation has been sent
    • A short note recording who the domain belongs to — see below

    For the last one, send FTCI a single line by email and keep a copy:

    “ftcihotels.org was registered on [date] by [your name] on behalf of, and belonging to, the Federation of Tibetan Cooperatives in India. The registration is held in FTCI's name and will be moved into FTCI's own account.”

    It takes a minute and costs nothing. If anyone ever asks who owns the web address — years from now, when the people involved may have changed — this one email settles it. Recording the intention is easy today and surprisingly hard to reconstruct later.

    Once those arrive we can finish the email setup the same day.
