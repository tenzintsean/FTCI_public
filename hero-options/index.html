Home page hero — two ways to keep the Potala

The Potala stays and nothing darkens it. Today three layers do — a 10% mute, a permanent blue-black wash, and the intro's black scrim — and the white lettering only works because of them. We tested putting dark lettering on the clean photograph instead: it fails, and not for a reason any amount of tuning fixes (shown below). That leaves one real question for you to decide — how much of the page the Potala gets.

  • Three layers sit on the Potala: the image is dropped to 90% over a blue field, a permanent blue-black wash covers it top to bottom (55% → 70%), and the intro adds a black radial scrim for 6.5 seconds.
  • Only the scrim fades. The wash never does — the Potala stays darkened for as long as the page is open.
  • The headline is also held back for 4.7 seconds while the intro plays.
  • White lettering on the darkened image readable — but only because of the darkening.

  • The image is clean — all three darkening layers removed, full brightness and colour. That part works.
  • But the words do not. Average contrast reads 6.5:1, which looks like a pass. Measured against the darkest pixel under the text it is 1.05:1 — letters disappear.
  • The Potala has near-black detail — window recesses, doorways, shadow — scattered through every part of the frame. Parts of each letterform land on it and vanish. We tested four different crops; the worst case stayed between 1.05:1 and 1.10:1 in all of them.
  • Not fixable by moving or resizing the text. The only ways to put words on this photograph are to darken it (rejected) or to lay a plate behind them — which still covers the Potala.

  • The Potala carries nothing. No wash, no lettering, no buttons — the photograph is simply seen, whole, the way an image of the Potala would be shown on its own.
  • Brand and headline move into a cream band directly beneath, in maroon and near-black well past the standard.
  • More of the palace is visible: with no text to accommodate, the crop can breathe. Still a crop, though — the top and bottom of the photograph are cut off.
  • The trade: a quieter, more formal opening. The photograph is a portrait rather than a backdrop, and the page reads as respectful rather than cinematic.
  • The booking search sits directly under the band — no scrolling needed on a normal laptop.

  • Nothing is cropped. The photograph is 1920×1000; at the site's width its natural height is 750px, so this shows it complete — the full sky, the full plaza, the whole palace, exactly as the photographer framed it.
  • Nothing on it, nothing over it, nothing cut from it. The strongest reading of "the Potala is our identity".
  • The trade: the image takes 750px of the opening screen, so on a typical laptop the booking search sits just at or below the fold — a visitor scrolls once before booking. That is a real cost on a site whose job is direct bookings.
  • If the photograph is ever replaced with one of a different shape, this layout adapts — the height simply follows the image rather than a fixed crop.
